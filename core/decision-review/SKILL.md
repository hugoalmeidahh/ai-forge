# Decision review

Review the decision within its actual stakes and reversibility. Do not append a generic reflection checklist to every response. Work from the proposal, evidence and requested scope.

1. State the decision and success condition in the author's terms.
2. Separate observed facts, stakeholder requirements, estimates and assumptions. Identify the few assumptions that could change the choice.
3. Consider credible alternatives and disconfirming evidence. Use concrete counterexamples, not speculative lists of every possible risk.
4. Examine likely failure modes, who notices, reversibility and recovery. Distinguish blocking uncertainty from a reversible choice.
5. Recommend the smallest experiment or evidence needed to resolve material uncertainty; do not restart an approved discovery phase without cause.

Deliver a clear recommendation, key trade-offs and remaining uncertainty. Preserve user authority and existing authorization; this review does not create a new approval gate for routine work.
